**Short answer: no.** For a flight on a U.S. airline, a psychiatric service dog does not need a PSD letter, a registry ID, or a paid certificate.

## What the airline can actually ask for

The airline can ask for a current, completed U.S. DOT [Service Animal Air Transportation Form](/blog/dot-service-animal-form). "Current" means you filled it out on or after the date you bought the ticket. If a flight is scheduled for 8 hours or more, it can also ask for the DOT relief attestation, which confirms how your dog will handle relief on a long flight.

At the airport, staff can ask two questions: whether the dog is needed because of a disability, and what work or task it's trained to perform. They can't ask about your disability, and they can't ask the dog to demonstrate its task.

## Why the letter doesn't help

Both DOT forms are free to download. A letter from a website doesn't replace them, and an ESA letter doesn't turn an animal into a service dog. If your dog isn't task-trained, it can fly as a pet, and that's a perfectly good trip too. [A service dog, a PSD, and an ESA are not the same thing at the airport.](/blog/service-dog-vs-esa-vs-psd-flying)
