Three labels get mixed up all the time: service dog, PSD, and ESA. At the airport they lead to different trips.

## Service dog

Under the U.S. Department of Transportation rule, a service animal is a dog, of any breed or type, that is individually trained to do work or perform tasks for a person with a disability. That includes physical, sensory, psychiatric, intellectual, and other mental disabilities.

A service dog flies in the cabin with you. The airline can ask for the official DOT service-animal form. That form is on your Travel Pass after you [Plan my trip](/assessment) and pay.

## PSD (psychiatric service dog)

A PSD is a service dog. The trained task supports a psychiatric disability. The rules are the same as for any other service dog. [You don't need a letter or a certificate.](/blog/do-you-need-a-psd-letter)

## ESA (emotional support animal)

The DOT rule says emotional support animals, comfort animals, and companion animals are not service animals. On U.S. airlines an ESA generally flies as a pet.

## What about cats, or dogs in training?

The rule covers dogs only, and service animals in training are not covered. A cat, or a dog still in training, can usually still fly as a pet.

## What PetPassGo will and won't do

We don't sell, sign, or attest a service-animal form, and we never ask you to buy a certificate or a letter. Your Travel Pass uses the official DOT form after you pay.
